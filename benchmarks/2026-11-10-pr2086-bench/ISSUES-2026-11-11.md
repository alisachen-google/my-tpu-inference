## I4. `write_back: backup failed under host memory pressure but subtree drop declined (node locked)` on the on arm

- Symptom: on the write-back **on** arm (`j-74143469-8c3a-4bd6-9575`) the prefill logged 96 such lines (12 per TP
  rank), in three bursts: 6 at 00:08:54 during the 12-session recall return (the point whose hit was 37.1%), 1 at
  00:12 during the 16-session return, 5 at 00:29:43 during the integrity run's recall phase. The off arm
  (`j-ae4dd88f-6893-4c7d-8796`) logged none. The line is one of the five invalidating patterns of the brief
  ("subtree drop declined").
- First log line (TP0): `[2026-11-11 00:08:54 TP0 EP0] write_back: backup failed under host memory pressure but
  subtree drop declined (node locked); root node 646 stays device-resident until host space frees`, preceded by
  `Prefill batch, #new-seq: 2, #new-token: 8185, #cached-token: 139264, #host-hit-token: 139264, full token usage:
  0.99`.
- Reading: with the switch on, a push in flight host-locks the whole path of the pushed leaf until the decode has
  stored it; when write-back then needs host room for a new backup and the LRU victim is on a locked path, the
  eviction is refused and the device copy is kept. No request failed, no out-of-memory followed (the H4 shape of
  2026-11-07 did not recur), and the counters show every push stored (204/204) and landed (204/204). The device pool
  was at 0.99 when the first burst happened, so the refused drops cost device room during that return.
- Whether it is a PR defect: a warning path that the PR's own recall commit was designed to make survivable
  (`adopt_received_backups`); it did not fail here. It is the one engine-side condition that separates the two arms
  during the integrity run (I5), so it is reported with that.
- Status: observed, not fixed, write-back specific: neither write-through arm (`j-ca202e3d-91f2-44d7-991e`,
  `j-8c1912b8-b43d-463f-8d17`) logged one. The 2P2D write-back on job (`j-dd3f6030-3b7a-4588-a5cd`) logged 40 (5 on
  TP0: prefill-0 at 04:06:06 during the T1 return and twice at 04:21:43 during chain leg 1; prefill-1 at 04:09:26
  during the T2 return and 04:22:24 during chain leg 2), again only during returns that recall, again with no failed
  request. The fresh-engine integrity run (I5) logged none and still diverged, so this condition is not what
  separates the integrity arms.
- Head 83198bc912: still present and more frequent, because the prefill now keeps a host window for every evicted
  node and pushes every evicted node (574 pushes on the 1P1D on arm against 204 on the first head), so the host tier
  is fuller when a return pins a recalled prefix. Counts: 168 lines on the write-back 1P1D on arm
  (`j-4b873230-04db-418e-969d`, 21 per rank; TP0 bursts 1 at 09:11:49 in the 12-session return, 14 at 09:22:43 in
  the mixed block, 6 at 09:26:53 in the integrity recall phase), 144 on the 2P2D on arm (`j-81cfa513-52b7-47f1-b22c`,
  112 on prefill-0 and 32 on prefill-1, during the T1, T2 and chain returns), 8 on the fresh-engine integrity run
  (`j-cd086d8e-ccfa-4a6c-a82b`, one event), 0 on every off arm and on both write-through arms. Still no failed
  request, no OOM, counters equal on all ranks, and the integrity runs that logged it are now 16 of 16, so its cost
  is device pages held during a return, not correctness.
- Code trace (where the lock comes from): `unified_radix_cache.py::_evict_components` (upstream) logs the line when
  the write-back backup of a device leaf fails for lack of host room and `unified_tree_core.py::drop_subtree_no_host`
  declines because the leaf or a descendant has `lock_ref` or `host_lock_ref` set. The PR holds host locks in three
  places: the recalled prefix node is pinned from the pull until its request leaves the queues
  (`prefill_dram_prefix_puller.py`, `_RecalledPrefixPin`, plus `lock_host_path` on the path while the pull is in
  flight), a push in flight locks the pushed leaf's path until the decode stored it (`prefill_dram_victim_pusher.py`),
  and the copy-ahead window pins the host copies of the next eviction victims (`hicache_write_back_copy_ahead.py`).
  The bursts coincide with returns that recall, which matches the first of these: the pulled 139k host-only node sits
  under the device prefix the eviction wants to back up, the host pool is full after the pull, and the drop is
  declined because the child is pinned.

## I5. Single-request output equivalence on the write-back on arm: 12 of 16 after the ladders, 13 of 16 on fresh engines (PR; closed on head 83198bc912)

- Procedure (`victim_integrity.py`, the E1 case of the reference): 16 sessions of 128k prefix + 8k new tokens filled
  at concurrency 1 with 6 concurrent 8k probes during fill and settle; then every session returns one request at a
  time with no probes (the recall phase); both engines are flushed and every return is recomputed from scratch (the
  verify phase); the 64 greedy output tokens of recall and verify are compared.
- Results, write-back, switch on, three independent runs:

  | Run | Engines | Exact | Diverging sessions (first differing token) | How the diverging sessions were served | `subtree drop declined` lines |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | `j-74143469-8c3a-4bd6-9575`, after the ladders | used | 12 / 16 | 6 (5), 11 (5), 14 (19), 15 (22) | 6, 11, 14 from host after recall; 15 not cached at all | 96 |
  | `j-c909ba56-55e8-4b10-8eb3`, attempt 1 (01:59-02:04Z, node lost after 8 verifies) | fresh | 7 / 8 | 6 (5) | host after recall | 0 |
  | `j-c909ba56-55e8-4b10-8eb3`, attempt 3 (02:25-02:32Z) | fresh | 13 / 16 | 6 (5), 9 (5), 11 (5) | host after recall | 0 |

  Session 6 diverges at token 5 in all three runs and session 11 in both complete runs. Token 5 is the first
  content-dependent output token (tokens 0-4 are the same for every session), so the recall path yields logits that
  differ slightly from the recompute and the argmax flips only in the sessions whose top two candidates are close.
  The divergence is therefore deterministic per prompt, not noise, and not a function of engine age.
- Controls, same procedure:
  - write-back, switch off (`j-ae4dd88f-6893-4c7d-8796`): 16 / 16, all 16 returns served from the prefill's **device**
    cache (139,264 device-cached tokens each, no host tier involved; an earlier version of this note said host, which
    was wrong);
  - write-through, switch on (`j-ca202e3d-91f2-44d7-991e`): 16 / 16, 15 of 16 served from the prefill's **host** tier
    after the recall from decode DRAM, 1 from device;
  - write-through, switch off (`j-8c1912b8-b43d-463f-8d17`): 16 / 16, all from device.
  So the recall from decode DRAM and the host-tier serving of a recalled prefix are exact under write-through, and the
  divergence needs write-back plus the switch. On the fresh on-arm run every one of the 16 returns pulled 122,880
  tokens plus a 128-token SWA window from decode DRAM (16 pulls, 1,966,080 + 2,048 tokens, counters equal on 8 ranks),
  the remaining 16,384 tokens of each prefix came from the prefill's own host copy; 14 returns were then served
  with 139,264 host-cached tokens and 2 (sessions 4 and 15) found nothing cached and recomputed in full (and were exact).
- Background probes (6 concurrent 8k requests during fill and settle) disagree with their own reference in 16 of 89
  cases at the same positions on every arm: TPU greedy decoding is not batch-invariant, which is why the recall phase
  runs one request at a time and why a divergence must be reproducible to count.
- What is specific to write-back with the switch on, from the tree (`sglang_tpu/`): with the switch on, a PD prefill's
  host tier keeps only the last SWA window of each path (`pd_decode_swa_hicache.py`, `_collect_unbacked_swa_nodes`:
  SWA nodes outside the live window of a path are tombstoned at eviction without a copy), and the host copy of a path is
  made at eviction time by the copy-ahead window (`hicache_write_back_copy_ahead.py`) rather than at prefill time as
  under write-through; the recall inserts the pulled prefix as a host-only node together with the window that ends
  where the pushed prefix ends (`disaggregation/decode_dram_offload/prefill_dram_prefix_puller.py`). The candidate this
  points at is the SWA window the return needs at its match end (the last 128 tokens before token 139,264), which under
  write-back comes from a copy taken at eviction time while under write-through it was copied when it was computed; a
  window copied after its SWA rows were recycled, or a window anchored to a different path end than the match end,
  would give exactly this picture (full-attention KV right, SWA window slightly wrong, small logit shifts). Not
  verified in code here.
- Reproducer: `STAGE=edges EDGES=E1` of the recipe with `WRITE_POLICY=write_back SWITCH=true MODEL_PROFILE=gpt-oss-120b`
  on fresh engines (the harness's `victim_integrity.py` with 16 sessions, 128k+8k, concurrency 1 in the recall phase);
  sessions 6 and 11 diverge at token 5. Raw outputs: `results/wb_on_e1fresh/g2086_edges_write_back_integrity.json`.
- PR defect: as far as this harness can tell, yes: a reproducible, prompt-deterministic output difference between a

  either engine. Open for the author; the write-through configuration is exact.

## I7. `Detected a stale or broken JIT extension for hicache_hash_cpp ... clearing its cache and retrying once` on every pull (PR, minor; closed on head 83198bc912)

- Symptom: on the switch-on arms the prefill logs this warning once per rank per recall pull: 560 lines (70 on TP0) on
  the write-back on arm for 71 pulls, 936 (117 on TP0) on the write-through on arm for 118 pulls, 128 (16 on TP0) on
  the fresh-engine integrity run for 16 pulls; the decode logs it at the same moments. Neither off arm logs it.
- First log line: `[2026-11-11 00:12:30 TP0 EP0] Detected a stale or broken JIT extension for hicache_hash_cpp at
  /root/.cache/torch_extensions/py312_cpu/hicache_hash_cpp; clearing its cache and retrying once.`
- Cause: `python/sglang/srt/utils/cpp_extension_loader.py` (`load_extension_with_recovery`) catches a load failure of
  the `hicache_hash_cpp` extension, deletes its build directory and rebuilds it; the rebuilt extension loads (no
  exception follows), but the next pull fails to load it again, so every pull pays a rebuild. With 8 ranks sharing the
  build directory, each rank's rebuild plausibly invalidates the others'.
- Effect: latency on the pull path (the rebuild happens inside the recall), no correctness effect seen (the
  write-through on arm is 16 / 16 exact with the same lines). Not pursued further here.
- PR defect: yes, minor (a per-pull rebuild of a C++ extension), or an image issue if the extension is meant to be
  prebuilt.
- **Head 83198bc912: closed.** Commit `83198bc912` ("Hash recalled pages only when the tree keeps page hashes")
  computes the hashes only with an L3 storage backend or an external cache linker, so the extension is never loaded on
  a recall here: 0 lines on every arm of the rebenchmark (write-back on 103 pulls, write-through on 127, 2P2D on 43,
  fresh-engine integrity 21).

## I8. `Raiden host transfer <id>#px / #pxs failed (DEADLINE_EXCEEDED, await_pull): Transfer session timed out` on the decode after a declined offer (PR, minor, new on head 83198bc912)

- Symptom: the write-through on arm's decode (`j-c1ee04fe-4082-4d27-9bbc`) logged, once per rank, at 08:59:45:
  `[2026-11-11 08:59:45 TP0 EP0] Raiden host transfer 5818794399394046034#px failed (DEADLINE_EXCEEDED, await_pull):
  Transfer session timed out` and the same for `#pxs` (16 lines; the harness counts `DEADLINE_EXCEEDED` as an
  invalidating pattern, so the arm's log scan shows `decode-0=16`). No other arm logged it.
- Context: it fell in the wipe between the 12-session return (ended 08:58:18) and the 16-session fill. The prefill's
  recall counters at 08:59:41 show `no_memory=1`: one offer was declined by the prefill for lack of memory and never
  pulled. The decode had already set up the transfer session for that offer (prefix `px` and window `pxs`) and waited
  for the pull; the decode's `flush_cache` meanwhile reported `Cache not flushed because there are pending requests.
  #queue-req: 0, #running-req: 0` at 08:59:29 and 08:59:39, then the session hit its 60 s deadline at 08:59:45 and the
  flush went through at 08:59:49.
- Effect: a declined offer holds a transfer session on the decode until the deadline, which here delayed a flush by
  about 20 s; no request failed and the 16-session fill and return were unaffected (88.5% hit, 10.7x). Under load a
  decode with many declined offers would carry as many dangling sessions for 60 s each.
- PR defect: minor; the offer path could cancel the decode-side session when the prefill declines, instead of letting
  it time out.
